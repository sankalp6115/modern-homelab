In my first year of college, I built a music player and called Melodious as a student project. I polished it for a whole semester, then abandoned it - which is honestly what happens to most student projects.
An assignment in the fourth semester got me back into it. We were working with virtual machines and linux and it opened up this whole idea of self-hosting - running your own software, on your own hardware, your way. 
I decided to actually finish what I started. I rebuilt the whole thing. I ported the whole stack into React frontend, FastAPI backend and SQLite database. I opted for a clean client-server architecture. SQLite felt right for a personal single-user app - simple, low overhead, easy to swap later when scalability becomes necessary.
The server is an old laptop on which i booted Alpine Linux sitting in my room. Everything on my home network can access it.
I use it every day now. More than 300 tracks, no ads, no platform deciding what gets recommended to me. I also built a custom shuffle that generates a full session queue so nothing repeats and nothing gets quietly buried - which was a specific frustration I had with every streaming app I've used.
The operational side has been the real learning curve. Deployments are still manual right now. I'm working toward CI/CD, Docker, and secure-tunneling to make the system accessible outside the network.
There's something different about maintaining software you depend on versus submitting it for a grade.
Open to feedback and suggestions—especially on how this could be made more robust and scalable.
